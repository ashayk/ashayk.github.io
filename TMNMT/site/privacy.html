Privacy policy

Your tinnitus frequency never leaves your phone.

Applies to Tinnitus Aid Pro for iPhone and iPad · Effective [DATE]

In short

The app has no accounts and asks for no personal details. Everything it learns about your hearing stays on the device: the frequency your tinnitus sits at, every answer you gave in the matching test, the loudness levels that were calibrated, and your listening history. None of it is sent anywhere, and we have no way to see it. Your music is read from your own library and never leaves the device. What we do receive is anonymous crash reports and anonymous counts of which screens get used, which carry none of the above.

1. Who we are

This policy covers the Tinnitus Aid Pro app for iPhone and iPad (“the app”), published by [DEVELOPER NAME] (“we”, “us”). It describes what the app handles, what leaves your device and what does not, and what choices you have. Questions can be sent to [CONTACT EMAIL].

2. What stays on your device

The app is built around a measurement of your hearing, which is health information about one identifiable person: you. It is treated accordingly. The following are stored on the device, in the app's own storage, and are never transmitted to us or to anyone else:

  • Your filter frequencies. Whether you arrived at them through the matching test or entered them by hand, and whether you keep one or several.
  • Everything the matching test records. Each pair of tones you were played, which one you chose, the loudness you set for each, and how far through a series you are.
  • Your hearing profile. The highest frequency you could hear during the test, which is used to set the range the test searches.
  • Your listening history. The per-day record of playback time, and the daily and weekly averages drawn from it.
  • The per-track analysis. The app measures each track once so the filter can be applied without clipping, and caches the result against that track.

Deleting the app removes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else, which also means there is nothing for us to restore if you delete it by accident.

3. Your music

The app asks for permission to read your music library so it can play your own tracks through the filter. That permission is read-only and is used for nothing else. Track titles, artists, album art and media identifiers are used on the device to show you what is playing and to remember which track was analyzed; none of them are sent to us. The audio itself is filtered on the device as it plays. It is never uploaded, and no copy of it is made.

If you decline the permission, the rest of the app still works, but there is nothing for it to play.

4. Processing on your device

All of the signal processing happens on your iPhone or iPad: measuring each track, building the filter, applying it in real time, and drawing the spectrum you see while it plays. None of this requires a network connection and none of it reaches a server of ours, because there is no server of ours in the path.

5. Anonymous crash reports and usage counts

Two third-party services receive information from the app, and neither receives anything from the list in section 2.

Crash reports are handled by Sentry. When the app crashes, hangs, or hits an error it can recover from, it sends the technical details of what went wrong: the version of the app, the device model and operating system version, and where in the code it happened. We use these to fix faults.

Usage counts are handled by TelemetryDeck, which is designed to work without identifying anybody. The app sends the shape of how it is used and nothing more: that onboarding was shown, which of the two routes to a filter was taken, that a filter was created or deleted and which of the two kinds it was, how far through a matching series you have got as a count out of the total, that playback started and how many tracks were queued, and that a track could not be read.

Specifically, and by design, these events never carry your tinnitus frequency, anything from your hearing profile, any individual answer or loudness setting from the matching test, any track title, artist or media identifier, or any identifier for you or your device beyond the anonymous per-app value TelemetryDeck derives for itself.

6. Sharing your history, if you choose to

The History screen has a share button, which hands your listening record to the iOS share sheet as plain text so you can send it to an audiologist or keep a copy. That happens only when you tap it, and where it goes from there is entirely your choice. The app does not send it anywhere on its own.

7. Purchases

Any purchase or subscription is processed by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment details, card number or billing address. What we receive from Apple is confirmation that a purchase is valid.

8. Health information

A measured tinnitus frequency and a hearing profile may be considered health information, and may be regulated as such where you live. The app keeps them on the device rather than offering an account, a backup or a sync. In short, such data never leaves the device.

9. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, or the equivalent minimum age where you live, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. As described above, the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles data, we will update this page and the effective date above. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
