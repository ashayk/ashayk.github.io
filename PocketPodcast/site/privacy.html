Privacy policy

Your recordings stay on your device. We don't know who you are.

Applies to Pocket Podcast (iPhone and iPad) and PodMerge (Mac) · Effective [DATE]

In short: the apps do not collect any personally identifiable information. Editing, speaker identification and export happen on your device. If you turn on the optional cloud features, your data goes directly from your device to that vendor under your own account — it never passes through servers of ours. The only thing we receive is anonymous crash reports, which you can turn off.

1. Who we are

This policy covers the Pocket Podcast app for iPhone and iPad and the PodMerge app for Mac (together, "the apps"), published by [DEVELOPER NAME] ("we", "us"). It describes what information the apps handle, where it goes, and what choices you have. Questions can be sent to [CONTACT EMAIL].

2. What we do not collect

We do not collect, store or transmit any personally identifiable information. Specifically, we never receive:

  • your name, email address, phone number or any account details — the apps have no accounts;
  • your videos, audio, photos, transcripts, clips or exported files;
  • the names you give speakers, episodes or files;
  • your location, contacts, calendar or any other data from your device;
  • your API keys for third-party services (see section 5).

3. Processing on your device

The core of the apps runs locally. Importing and recording video, identifying who is speaking, assigning speakers to cameras, color grading, trimming, noise reduction, loudness normalization, thumbnails and export are all performed on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. Nothing about the content of your recordings leaves the device unless you switch on one of the optional cloud features described in section 5.

The apps ask for permission to use the camera, microphone and photo library only when you use the corresponding feature (recording, or importing from your Camera Roll). Under iOS's limited photo access, the apps can only see the items you select.

4. Anonymous crash reports

To find bugs, the apps may send depersonalised crash reports to one third-party service:

ServiceWhat it receivesProvider's policy
Sentry (crash reporting)Crash reports and error traces: the app version, operating system version, device model, and the code path that failed. Personal data, screenshots and view hierarchies are not attached.sentry.io/privacy

These reports are anonymous. They carry no user identifier, account or email address, no IP address is stored, and they contain no content from your recordings, transcripts, file names, speaker names or API keys. Neither we nor Sentry can connect a report to a person or to a specific device; we can only see that some installation of a given version crashed in a given place. We do not run usage analytics; the numbers that appear in the apps (for example, the estimated cost of a cloud step) are calculated on the device and are not reported.

You can opt out at any time in the app's Settings; opting out takes effect immediately and applies to all future sessions.

5. Optional cloud features and your own keys

Two features use external services. Both are off until you turn them on, and both use an API key that you obtain from the vendor under your own account:

  • ElevenLabs Scribe for speaker identification with a word-level transcript. When you run it, the audio of the episode is uploaded from your device to ElevenLabs.
  • OpenRouter for choosing clips and writing titles, descriptions, chapters and tags. When you run it, the text of the transcript is sent from your device to OpenRouter, which routes it to the model. The apps use Anthropic's Claude Sonnet through OpenRouter, and every request is sent with OpenRouter's data-collection setting denied — so it is only routed to providers that do not retain prompts or use them for training, regardless of your account's defaults. The transcript is processed to produce the result and is not kept by the model provider.

These transfers go directly from your device to the vendor. They do not pass through, and are not visible to, any server operated by us. We do not see the data you send, the results you get back, or your keys. Each vendor processes that data under its own terms and privacy policy, which you accepted when you created your account with them: ElevenLabs, OpenRouter, Anthropic. Charges for these services are billed by the vendor to your account, not by us.

Your API keys are stored in the device's Keychain, shown as hidden text in Settings, and sent only to the vendor they belong to. Deleting the app removes them.

6. Where your files live

Exports, transcripts, clips and metadata are written to a folder inside the app's own storage on your device (visible in the Files app on iPhone and iPad, or wherever you choose to save on Mac). They are yours: sharing them, backing them up through iCloud or a computer, or deleting them is entirely under your control and is not tracked by us.

7. Protected health information

The apps are not designed for, and must not be used with, recordings that contain protected health information or other data subject to healthcare privacy regulation, particularly when the optional cloud features are enabled.

8. Children

The apps are not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your jurisdiction) and we do not knowingly collect information from them. As described above, the apps do not collect personal information from anyone.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change how the apps handle data, we will update this page and the effective date above. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.

10. Contact
